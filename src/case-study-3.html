Context & Ownership

Set the scene in a few sentences: the product, the team, and where things stood when you arrived. Keep it to what a reader needs to understand the rest of the page, not the full company history.

Then state what you owned, plainly. Which decisions were yours, which were shared, and which were made above you. Ownership is what makes the rest of the case believable.

Problem

Name the problem as the user experienced it, then as the business experienced it. One sentence each is usually enough; the artefact below does the persuading.

If there is a number that made the team take it seriously, put it here and say where it came from.

Discovery

What you did to understand the problem, and what you learned. Lead with the insight, then the decision it changed. Method is supporting detail, not the story.

Process

Show the options you seriously considered and why you chose one. Senior work is visible in what you rejected and the reason, so give each option a real verdict.

Solution

The final state, in a few sentences. Say what the design does and how it answers the problem from earlier; the screens and their annotations carry the detail.

Evolution

What changed after launch and why. This is about the design over time, not its final state: what you learned from real use and what you did about it.
